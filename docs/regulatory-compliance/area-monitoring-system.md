# Regulatory Compliance: Area Monitoring System (AMS) Requirements

## Standard
The Area Monitoring System (AMS) requirement stems from:
- **Article 70(1), Regulation (EU) 2021/2116** — Member States must establish and
  operate AMS from 1 January 2023, with the permitted gradual implementation governed
  by the implementing rules.
- **Article 10, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/1173** — defines coverage and the
  rollout of monitorable conditions. In the original text, paragraph 3 extends the
  definition to conditions monitorable with geotagged photos from 1 January 2025;
  paragraph 4 permits gradual inclusion and requires at least 70% of interventions
  with photo-only monitorable conditions to be covered before 1 January 2027.
  Paragraph 9 provides the 2023 starting scope.

These references describe the original EU framework. Before operational use, confirm
the consolidated rules and Hungary's approved intervention/year rollout; do not
interpret 2025 as a universal farmer-photo requirement or all checks becoming
satellite-monitorable.

## Scope
AMS is defined as the regular and systematic observation, tracking, and assessment of agricultural activities using Copernicus Sentinel satellite data (or data of at least equivalent value).
- Relies primarily on **Sentinel-1** (radar) and **Sentinel-2** (optical) time series.
  Acquisition cadence depends on mission, constellation, orbit, and location; usable
  parcel observations also depend on cloud, masking, and quality.
- Enables automated analysis (mowing, ploughing, crop cover change detection) to reduce reliance on manual on-the-spot checks.
- Feeds directly into the **Integrated Administration and Control System (IACS)** and the **Land Parcel Identification System (LPIS)**.
- Covers area-based interventions under IACS and their monitorable conditions,
  including basic income support, area-specific constraints, and relevant eco-schemes.
  PAS and SIM in the Irish source are national examples, not Hungarian intervention
  names. Hungary's mapping must follow its CAP Strategic Plan and scheme-year rules.
- Member States must annually assess system quality using an EU-level methodology.

## Relevance to Case Study 47 (Hungarian Paying Agency)
This is a core driver of the transformation objective: the case brief describes
coverage gaps under a sample-based approach. The proposed Parcel Monitoring Agent
supports population-wide analysis of monitorable conditions, with explicit
abstention, complementary evidence, and independent quality validation. Other
conditions still require appropriate controls. Reduced financial corrections are a
benefit hypothesis to validate, not a guaranteed consequence of satellite coverage.
